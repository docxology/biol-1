## Module 12 — Darwin and evolution

### Focus ideas

- **Descent with modification** (change over time from shared ancestors).  
- **Natural selection:** heritable **variation**, **overproduction** of offspring, **differential reproductive success** (fitness = relative reproductive success).  
- **Individuals** do not evolve; **populations** change in allele frequency over generations.  
- **Evidence** from fossils, biogeography, comparative anatomy (homology), molecular comparisons.

## Module 13 — How populations evolve (microevolution)

### Focus ideas

- **Microevolution** = **change in allele frequencies** in a population over time.  
- **Five mechanisms:** mutation, **gene flow**, **genetic drift**, non-random mating (e.g. sexual selection), **natural selection**.  
- **Drift** matters most in **small** populations; **bottleneck** vs **founder** effects.  
- **Selection modes** on a continuous trait: **directional**, **stabilizing**, **disruptive**.  
- **Hardy–Weinberg** = mathematical baseline of **no** evolution; real populations usually violate it.

## Module 14 — Macroevolution and speciation

### Focus ideas

- **Biological species concept** (interbreed in nature → viable fertile offspring; **reproductive isolation** from other such groups).  
- **Prezygotic** barriers (before zygote): habitat, **temporal**, behavioral, mechanical, gametic, etc.  
- **Postzygotic** barriers (after zygote): hybrid viability, hybrid fertility, hybrid breakdown.  
- **Allopatric** speciation: geographic split then divergence. **Sympatric** speciation: same place (e.g. **polyploidy** in plants).

## Module 15 — Population and systems ecology

### Focus ideas

- **Exponential (J)** vs **logistic (S)** growth; **carrying capacity (K)**.  
- **Density-dependent** (competition, disease, predation often intensify with crowding) vs **density-independent** (e.g. many fires, some storms) regulation.  
- **Trophic levels** and **~10% energy** transfer; **energy flows**, **nutrients** cycle.  
- **Producers, consumers, decomposers**; food chains limited in length partly because of energy loss as **heat** and work.
