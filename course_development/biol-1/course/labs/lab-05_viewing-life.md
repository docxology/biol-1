# Lab 5: Viewing Life — Observing Structure and Function

## Overview

Biology is the study of life. In this lab, you will move beyond the textbook and observe living systems directly. Using a compound light microscope, you will examine a variety of specimens—from plant cells to pond water organisms—to identify common features of life and appreciate its diversity. This lab focuses on **observation**, **scientific illustration**, and **connecting structure to function**.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lab, you will be able to:

1. Use a compound microscope to locate and focus on living specimens.
2. Create accurate scientific drawings of biological specimens.
3. Identify common structures (cell walls, chloroplasts, cytoplasm) in different organisms.
4. Relate observe structures to their functions (e.g., green chloroplasts = photosynthesis).
5. Appreciate the complexity and diversity of life at the microscopic scale.

## Materials

* Compound light microscope
* Slides and coverslips
* *Elodea* (freshwater plant) leaves
* Pond water samples (containing various protists/algae)
* Droppers/pipettes
* Lens paper

## Part 1: The Plant Cell — *Elodea*

*Elodea* is a common aquatic plant. Its leaves are only a few cells thick, making them perfect for viewing under a microscope.

**Procedure:**

1. Place a single *Elodea* leaf on a slide.
2. Add a drop of water and a coverslip.
3. Focus under **Low Power (4×)**, then move to **Medium (10×)** and **High Power (40×)**.

## Part 2: The Microscopic World — Pond Water

Pond water is a teeming ecosystem. You may see algae (green, non-moving or moving), protozoa (swimming, crawling), or small animals (rotifers, crustaceans).

**Procedure:**

1. Place a drop of pond water on a clean slide.
2. Add a coverslip carefully.
3. Scan the slide under **Low Power (4×)** to find movement or interesting objects.
4. Switch to higher power to observe details.
