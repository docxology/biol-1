## Part C: Free Response (9 points)

*Choose THREE out of five. Each question is worth 3 points.*
*Award partial credit as appropriate for incomplete but partially correct concepts.*

1. **Water: The Molecule of Life.**
   - *Key concepts:* Must identify two properties and brief biological examples.
   - Example 1: **Cohesion/Adhesion** (allows water transport up plant stems).
   - Example 2: **High Specific Heat / Temperature Moderation** (helps organisms maintain stable internal body temperatures; sweating cools body).
   - Example 3: **Universal Solvent** (dissolves salts, sugars, and nutrients for transport in blood/sap).
   - Example 4: **Density of Ice** (ice floats, insulating bodies of water from freezing solid so aquatic life can survive).

2. **The Flow of Genetic Information.**
   - *Key concepts:* Trace the path through specific organelles.
   - **Nucleus** (Manager, where DNA instructions are stored/transcribed).
   - **Ribosome** (Factory worker, often on the Rough ER, where the protein is assembled).
   - **Golgi apparatus** (Shipping center, modifies and packages the protein into a vesicle for secretion).

3. **Crossing the Border.**
   - *Key concepts:* Differentiate simple diffusion and active transport.
   - **Simple Diffusion:** Passive movement of molecules from high concentration to low concentration (down the gradient). Requires **no ATP**.
   - **Active Transport:** Movement of molecules from low concentration to high concentration (against the gradient). Requires the input of cellular energy / **ATP**.

4. **Energy Cycles.**
   - *Key concepts:* Connection between photosynthesis and cellular respiration.
   - Photosynthesis uses solar energy, CO₂, and H₂O to produce **Glucose and Oxygen**.
   - Those products (Glucose and Oxygen) become the reactants for Cellular Respiration.
   - Cellular Respiration breaks down the glucose using oxygen to create **ATP**, releasing **CO₂ and H₂O** as waste.
   - The waste of one process is the fuel/ingredient for the other, forming a continuous cycle of matter.

5. **Molecular Form and Function.**
   - *Key concepts:* Select a macromolecule, provide an example, and link structure to function.
   - **Lipids (e.g., Phospholipids):** Have a hydrophilic head and hydrophobic tails. This specific structure allows them to automatically form a bilayer in water, creating the semi-permeable cell membrane.
   - **Proteins (e.g., Enzymes):** Have a highly specific 3D shape containing an active site. This shape perfectly fits a specific substrate, allowing it to catalyze particular chemical reactions.
   - **Carbohydrates (e.g., Cellulose or Glycogen):** Cellulose forms long, straight, rigid chains perfect for structural support in plant cell walls. Glycogen is highly branched, allowing for quick enzyme access to release energy rapidly.
   - **Nucleic Acids (e.g., DNA):** The double helix structure with complementary base pairing allows it to be unzipped and easily accurately copied or read for genetic instructions.
