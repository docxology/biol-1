## Part C: Integrated Short Answer (25 points)

*Student submits **any five** of seven prompts; **5 points** each. Award partial credit.*

**1. Mitosis vs. meiosis.**

- **Mitosis:** one nuclear division (plus cytokinesis) → **2** daughter nuclei/cells; products **diploid** (for typical human somatic starting cell); daughter cells **genetically identical** to each other except mutation; roles include growth/repair/development (asexual reproduction in some organisms).
- **Meiosis:** **two** divisions → **4** haploid products (gametes in animals); **genetically non-identical** owing to crossing over, independent assortment; purpose sexual reproduction / genetic recombination in gametes.

**2. Gene → protein + epigenetics.**

- **Transcription** (DNA → RNA; nucleus in eukaryotes) → **Translation** (mRNA → polypeptide on ribosomes; cytoplasm/RER) → folding/modifications → functional protein.
- **Epigenetics hook:** promoter methylation dense chromatin or histone modifications can **reduce transcription** without changing DNA letter sequence.

**3. Natural selection + analogy vs homology.**

- **Selection:** heritable **variation**; **more offspring than environment supports** long-term (overproduction framing); **non-random survival/reproduction** tied to traits → allele-frequency shifts when genetic.
- **Homology:** shared ancestry, structure modified (may differ in function). **Analogy:** similar function/appearance from **convergent** evolution, not necessarily recent common ancestry for that trait.

**4. Drift vs. selection + bottleneck + founder.**

- **Drift:** random sampling of alleles; magnitude ↑ when **N** small; direction not inherently “for” adaptation.
- **Selection:** systematic differences in survival/reproduction tied to **heritable** traits.
- **Bottleneck:** catastrophe randomly eliminates most individuals; survivors’ allele pool may by chance differ strongly from before.
- **Founder:** small group colonizes new area; allele frequencies reflect **founders’** sampled genes.

**5. Exponential vs. logistic + limits.**

- **Exponential (J):** per capita growth high early when resources plentiful (idealized unlimited phase).
- **Logistic (S):** growth rate declines as **N** → **K**; **K** = carrying capacity (environment-supported equilibrium-ish ceiling).
- **Density-dependent:** disease spreading with crowding; competition for food when scarce—intensifies with density.
- **Density-independent:** storm/fire/freeze framing that kills similar fraction regardless of density (idealized textbook contrast).

**6. Photosynthesis vs. cellular respiration.**

- **Photosynthesis:** overview—uses light energy to build sugars from CO₂ and water in producers (chloroplasts in many eukaryotes); outputs O₂ as by-product in oxygenic overview.
- **Cellular respiration:** overview—oxidizes fuel (often glucose) to harvest usable energy with O₂ in many eukaryotes; mitochondria central for aerobic harvest.
- **Relationship:** products of one are substrates/context for the other at ecosystem scale; ATP/NADPH vs ATP from glucose contrasts acceptable at intro level.

**7. Species concept + isolation barriers.**

- **BSC (intro):** groups interbreeding (or potentially interbreeding) natural populations reproductively isolated from others; fertile/viable offspring framing as taught.
- **Prezygotic example:** temporal / behavioral / habitat / mechanical / gametic isolation blocking mating or fertilization **before** zygote.
- **Postzygotic example:** hybrid inviability, hybrid sterility (e.g., mule), or hybrid breakdown **after** zygote forms.

## Part D: Essay (15 points)

*One essay chosen. Holistic rubric suggestion: thesis & organization (**4**), accurate biology across units (**7**), use of vocabulary (**2**), conclusion (**2**). Adjust to course norms.*

### Option A — Molecules to populations

- DNA stores inherited information in genes; **alleles** are variant forms; **traits** are phenotypic outcomes influenced by genotype + environment + regulation.
- **Mutation** introduces new alleles; **selection** shifts frequencies when traits covary with reproductive success; **drift** randomly shifts frequencies (strong in small populations); **gene flow** homogenizes/diversifies via migration/mating between populations.
- **Populations evolve** = allele/gene-pool frequencies change across generations; individuals carry alleles but evolution is a population-level process over generations.

### Option B — Energy, matter, humans

- **Energy** enters as sunlight → producers fix chemical energy; passes **one-way** through consumers with **heat loss** at transfers; not recycled like atoms.
- **Nutrients** (N, P, etc.) move producer→consumer→**decomposer**→environment→producer cycles.
- **~10% rule** (order-of-magnitude): each step retains only a fraction of production → long chains need **more** primary production per calorie at apex than diets anchored on plants/primary consumers.

### Option C — Speciation

- **Biological species concept:** populations interbreeding in nature producing viable fertile offspring within the group; **reproductive isolation** between species-like clusters (limitations acknowledged at AP/intro level).
- **Prezygotic:** habitat/temporal/behavioral/mechanical/gametic examples—block mating/fertilization **before** zygote.
- **Postzygotic:** hybrid inviability / sterility / breakdown—zygote forms but gene flow blocked afterward.
- **Allopatric:** geography splits populations then divergence. **Sympatric:** divergence without geographic isolation—**polyploidy** classic plant example.

### Option D — Capstone systems synthesis

- Strong essays organize a scenario across **at least three scales**, such as DNA/gene expression, cell function, organism traits, population allele or abundance change, community interactions, and ecosystem energy/matter limits.
- Must include a real **feedback loop or constraint**, such as carrying capacity, density dependence, enzyme limits, membrane constraints, or trophic energy loss.
- Must name evidence that would test the explanation, such as trait measurements, survival/reproduction data, allele-frequency change, microscopy, gel/electrophoresis data, population counts, or ecosystem observations.
- Highest-credit answers explain links rather than merely listing vocabulary.
