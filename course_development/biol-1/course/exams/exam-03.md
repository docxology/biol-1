## Part A: Multiple Choice (30 points)

*Select the best answer for each question. Each question is worth 1 point.*

### Module 12: Darwin and Evolution

1. Darwin’s phrase **descent with modification** best captures the idea that:
    - A) Each species was created in its present form and cannot change
    - B) Only the strongest individual in a species updates the gene pool during its lifetime
    - C) Related lineages share ancestors and accumulate heritable change over many generations
    - D) Evolution requires organisms to choose beneficial mutations on purpose

2. In evolutionary biology, **fitness** is most appropriately defined as:
    - A) Relative contribution of genes to the next generation compared with others in the population
    - B) Maximum possible lifespan regardless of reproduction
    - C) Ability to learn new behaviors within one lifetime
    - D) Total body mass relative to other species

3. Which observation in Darwin’s reasoning corresponds to **overproduction of offspring**?
    - A) Parents vary in traits that can be inherited
    - B) Many descendants are produced, but not all survive and reproduce
    - C) Populations never change allele frequencies
    - D) Mutations always match what the environment “needs”

4. **Homologous** features such as the forelimb bones of a whale, a bat, and a human support evolution because they:
    - A) Show identical functions in every species
    - B) Reflect shared ancestry with structural modification for different roles
    - C) Prove that whales evolved from modern bats
    - D) Demonstrate that analogous traits never evolve

5. Convergent evolution produces superficially similar forms in distant lineages mainly because:
    - A) Gene flow merges unrelated species
    - B) Strong directional selection removes all variation instantly
    - C) Similar selection pressures favor similar phenotypes even without recent shared ancestry
    - D) Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium forces identical traits

6. Which statement best explains why biologists say **populations** evolve rather than **individuals**?
    - A) Individuals cannot change any traits during life
    - B) Evolution is measured as change in allele frequencies in a population across generations
    - C) Natural selection ignores heritable variation
    - D) Fitness applies only to entire ecosystems, not organisms

7. A tan deepens on human skin after UV exposure; the change is not reliably inherited at the same intensity by children who were never exposed. This scenario highlights:
    - A) Evolutionary fitness increasing in one generation
    - B) Acclimation within a lifetime versus allele-frequency change across generations
    - C) Founder effect in humans
    - D) Sympatric speciation

8. Related finch species on neighboring islands with differing beaks illustrate which broad line of evidence when paired with geographic patterns?
    - A) Comparative biochemistry only rules out fossils
    - B) Biogeography combined with descent with modification
    - C) Proof that drift never occurs on islands
    - D) Evidence that species definitions never use reproduction

### Module 13: How Populations Evolve

1. **Microevolution**, as used in this course, refers to:
    - A) Growth of one organism from juvenile to adult size
    - B) Change in allele frequencies in a population over time
    - C) Sudden origin of a new kingdom in one mating event
    - D) Replacement of DNA by RNA in every cell

2. Alleles enter or leave a population through migration and interbreeding with other populations in the process called:
    - A) Genetic drift
    - B) Founder effect only
    - C) Gene flow
    - D) Postzygotic isolation

3. A hurricane randomly kills most individuals in an insect population; survivors rebuild the population with altered allele frequencies largely due to chance sampling. This scenario fits:
    - A) Directional selection on camouflage every time
    - B) Bottleneck effect associated with genetic drift
    - C) Increased gene flow through the storm
    - D) Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium maintenance

4. A handful of rodents reach a new habitat and found a population whose gene pool reflects only the alleles those founders carried. This illustrates:
    - A) Bottleneck in the source population only
    - B) Founder effect (genetic drift)
    - C) Disruptive selection on teeth without variation
    - D) Sympatric polyploid speciation

5. When **intermediate** human birth weights survive best and **extremes** are selected against, the pattern is labeled:
    - A) Directional selection
    - B) Disruptive selection
    - C) Stabilizing selection
    - D) Genetic drift only

6. When **both extremes** of a trait survive and reproduce better than **intermediates**, the pattern is:
    - A) Stabilizing selection
    - B) Directional selection only
    - C) Disruptive selection
    - D) Founder effect

7. The Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium model is useful primarily because it provides:
    - A) Proof that evolution never occurs in nature
    - B) A mathematical baseline against which real populations can be compared
    - C) The formal definition of macroevolution only
    - D) Carrying capacity estimates for every species

8. Mutations originate new alleles; together with gene flow, genetic drift, non-random mating, and natural selection, they describe processes that can:
    - A) Guarantee allele frequencies never move from one generation to the next
    - B) Change allele frequencies in populations
    - C) Eliminate all variation immediately in large populations
    - D) Replace sunlight as ecosystem energy input

### Module 14: Macroevolution

1. Under the **biological species concept**, species are recognized largely by:
    - A) Matching color patterns in a field guide only
    - B) Identical chromosome numbers without breeding tests
    - C) Natural interbreeding within the group producing viable, fertile offspring and reproductive isolation from other such groups
    - D) Living in the same forest regardless of gene exchange

2. Two frog species breed in different months so hybrids rarely form. This barrier is:
    - A) Postzygotic hybrid breakdown
    - B) Prezygotic temporal isolation
    - C) Density-dependent regulation
    - D) Exponential growth limitation

3. Weak or inviable hybrid offspring illustrate:
    - A) Prezygotic behavioral isolation
    - B) Postzygotic reduced hybrid viability
    - C) Gene flow between populations
    - D) Logistic carrying capacity

4. A sterile mule from horse × donkey matings represents:
    - A) Prezygotic gametic incompatibility only
    - B) Sympatric polyploid speciation
    - C) Postzygotic reduced hybrid fertility
    - D) Founder effect in horses

5. A new geographic barrier divides one interbreeding fish species into two populations that diverge until they no longer mate. This exemplifies:
    - A) Sympatric speciation via polyploidy only
    - B) Allopatric speciation
    - C) Instant macroevolution without allele-frequency change
    - D) Density-independent mutualism

6. A plant lineage doubles its chromosome number and becomes reproductively isolated **without** geographic separation. This route commonly illustrates:
    - A) Allopatric divergence only
    - B) Sympatric speciation (for example via polyploidy)
    - C) Purely postzygotic prezygotic barriers
    - D) Trophic cascade elimination

7. In this course, **macroevolution** is best understood as:
    - A) A separate genetic mechanism unrelated to allele-frequency change
    - B) Larger-scale evolutionary outcomes built from the same population-level processes accumulated over long spans
    - C) Changes restricted to bacteria only
    - D) Synonymous with acclimation within individuals

### Module 15: Population and Systems Ecology

1. Unlimited-resource phases of population increase often follow a **J**-shaped curve described as:
    - A) Logistic growth approaching **K** immediately
    - B) Stable equilibrium without births or deaths
    - C) Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium
    - D) Exponential growth

2. In the logistic model, the symbol **K** stands for:
    - A) Carrying capacity
    - B) Mutation rate per generation
    - C) Number of trophic levels only
    - D) Hybrid sterility frequency

3. A contagious parasite spreads faster when hosts are crowded. That limiting factor is typically classified as:
    - A) Density-independent because parasites are small
    - B) Only abiotic regardless of biology
    - C) Density-dependent
    - D) Part of photosynthesis efficiency

4. A lightning fire kills a similar fraction of saplings in sparse and dense plots (idealized case where crowding does not change fire impact). Classified as a limiting disturbance in many texts, this behaves most like:
    - A) Density-dependent competition for mates only
    - B) An increase in **K** for every species
    - C) Conversion of heat back into chemical bond energy for producers
    - D) Density-independent mortality

5. The approximate **10% energy transfer** rule implies that supporting humans mainly on long marine food chains (many trophic steps) generally requires:
    - A) More primary production at the base because energy is lost between levels
    - B) Less primary production than eating plants directly
    - C) Equal joules stored at every trophic level by definition
    - D) Decomposers to create new sunlight inside consumers

6. In ecosystems, chemical nutrients such as nitrogen are recycled through producers and consumers, whereas energy captured by photosynthesis is ultimately:
    - A) Stored unchanged inside fossils forever with no loss
    - B) Returned photon-for-photon to chlorophyll in the same pathway as nutrient atoms
    - C) Lost largely as heat along transfers through trophic levels
    - D) Divided equally among all trophic levels without loss

7. Fungi and bacteria that reduce dead biomass and release usable nutrients back toward producers function ecologically as:
    - A) Primary producers that fix carbon using sunlight only in soil
    - B) Apex predators in every food chain
    - C) Agents that recycle energy from heat into new chemical bonds for autotrophs
    - D) Decomposers central to nutrient cycling
