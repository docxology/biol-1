# Module 13: How Populations Evolve - Practice Quiz

Use this low-stakes quiz after reviewing the module keys and learning questions.

1. Microevolution is defined as:
   - A. the origin of entirely new kingdoms of life
   - B. a change in allele frequencies in a population over generations
   - C. a change in a single individual's body
   - D. the extinction of every species
   - Answer: B
   - Why: Microevolution is a shift in allele frequencies within a population over time.

2. Genetic drift has its strongest effect in:
   - A. small populations
   - B. very large populations
   - C. populations with no variation
   - D. populations that never reproduce
   - Answer: A
   - Why: Random sampling changes allele frequencies most strongly when the population is small.

3. A few birds colonize a new island and start a population with limited variation. This reduced variation is due to:
   - A. natural selection alone
   - B. gene flow between islands
   - C. mutation pressure
   - D. the founder effect
   - Answer: D
   - Why: The founder effect is genetic drift that occurs when a new population begins from only a few individuals.

4. The Hardy-Weinberg principle is useful mainly because it:
   - A. proves evolution never happens
   - B. measures average body size
   - C. provides a non-evolving baseline to compare real populations against
   - D. edits alleles directly
   - Answer: C
   - Why: Hardy-Weinberg describes allele frequencies with no evolution. Deviations from it signal that evolutionary forces are at work.
