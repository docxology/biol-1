## Unit 1: The Chemistry of Life (Modules 01–06)

### Focus ideas

- **Module 01:** Life is recognized by shared properties (cells, energy use, response, reproduction, regulation, evolution). Scientific claims are tested with observations, variables, and repeatable reasoning.
- **Module 02:** Atoms form elements; covalent, ionic, and hydrogen bonds create different molecular properties. Water polarity explains cohesion, temperature buffering, and dissolving. pH tracks hydrogen ion concentration.
- **Module 03:** Carbon forms diverse skeletons. Dehydration synthesis builds polymers; hydrolysis breaks them. Carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids each serve distinct functions.
- **Module 04:** Cell theory — cells are the basic units of life. Prokaryotic vs eukaryotic organization. Organelles divide cellular labor.
- **Module 05:** Phospholipid bilayers create selective permeability. Passive transport (diffusion, osmosis) vs active transport. Tonicity predicts water movement.
- **Module 06:** ATP couples energy-releasing and energy-requiring reactions. Enzymes lower activation energy. Photosynthesis builds sugar; respiration harvests usable energy.

## Unit 2: Genetics (Modules 07–11)

### Focus ideas

- **Module 07:** DNA structure and base-pairing. Replication is semiconservative. Transcription produces RNA; translation builds proteins using codons. Mutations alter protein outcomes.
- **Module 08:** Mitosis produces identical daughter cells for growth/repair. Meiosis produces haploid gametes. Crossing over and independent assortment create variation. Nondisjunction changes chromosome number.
- **Module 09:** Alleles are gene variants. Mendelian inheritance predicts patterns. Punnett squares model probability. Incomplete dominance, codominance, polygenic traits, and environment complicate simple patterns.
- **Module 10:** Epigenetics — genes are switched on/off without changing the DNA sequence. DNA methylation and histone acetylation regulate expression. X-inactivation and environmental influences.
- **Module 11:** Genomics and biotechnology — restriction enzymes, PCR, gel electrophoresis, CRISPR-Cas9, and genetically modified organisms.

## Unit 3: Evolution & Ecology (Modules 12–16)

### Focus ideas

- **Module 12:** Darwin's "descent with modification." Natural selection requires variation, overproduction, and differential reproductive success. Evidence from fossils, biogeography, comparative anatomy, and molecular comparisons.
- **Module 13:** Microevolution = change in allele frequencies. Five mechanisms: mutation, gene flow, genetic drift, non-random mating, natural selection. Bottleneck vs founder effects. Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium as a null model.
- **Module 14:** Biological species concept. Prezygotic vs postzygotic barriers. Allopatric vs sympatric speciation.
- **Module 15:** Exponential (J) vs logistic (S) growth; carrying capacity (K). Density-dependent vs density-independent regulation. ~10% energy transfer per trophic level. Energy flows; nutrients cycle.
- **Module 16:** Capstone — biological explanations become stronger when they connect molecules, cells, organisms, populations, and ecosystems. Mechanism chains, feedback loops, constraints, and tradeoffs.
