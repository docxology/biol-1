# Module 10: Epigenetics - Practice Quiz

Use this low-stakes quiz after reviewing the module keys and learning questions.

1. How does epigenetic regulation differ from a mutation?
   - A. Both permanently change the DNA sequence.
   - B. Epigenetics deletes entire genes.
   - C. Epigenetics changes gene activity without changing the DNA sequence.
   - D. Mutations never affect any trait.
   - Answer: C
   - Why: Epigenetic marks such as methylation change how genes are expressed, not the underlying sequence. A mutation changes the sequence itself.

2. DNA methylation is usually associated with:
   - A. reduced transcription of a gene
   - B. faster DNA replication
   - C. deletion of a chromosome
   - D. building proteins directly
   - Answer: A
   - Why: Methylation typically silences or reduces transcription by making the DNA less accessible.

3. When chromatin is packed tightly, genes in that region are generally:
   - A. copied much faster
   - B. more likely to mutate
   - C. more accessible to be read
   - D. less accessible and less expressed
   - Answer: D
   - Why: Compact chromatin blocks access to genes, so they are expressed less. Open chromatin allows transcription.

4. Two genetically identical twins come to differ in a trait over their lifetimes. A good explanation is that:
   - A. their DNA sequences must actually be different
   - B. environment and epigenetic changes altered their gene expression
   - C. one twin is not truly human
   - D. epigenetic change is impossible
   - Answer: B
   - Why: Identical DNA can still show different expression because environment-driven epigenetic changes turn genes up or down.
