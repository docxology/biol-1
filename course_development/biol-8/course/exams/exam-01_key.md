### 1. Water and Life

**Key points (any 3 properties with hydrogen bonding + example):**

- **High specific heat**: H-bonds absorb heat energy before breaking → organisms maintain stable body temperature
- **Cohesion/adhesion**: H-bonds hold water molecules together → water transport in plant xylem (capillary action)
- **Solvent properties**: Polarity from H-bonds allows water to dissolve ionic/polar substances → transport of nutrients in blood
- **Density of ice**: H-bonds in ice form an open lattice making ice less dense → lakes freeze from top down, insulating aquatic life

### 2. The Protein Assembly Line

**Expected pathway:**

1. **Ribosomes on rough ER** — amino acid chain is synthesized and threaded into the ER lumen
2. **Rough ER** — protein is folded and may receive initial modifications (e.g., sugar groups)
3. **Transport vesicle** — budded from ER, carries protein to the Golgi
4. **Golgi apparatus** — further modifies, sorts, and packages the protein into a secretory vesicle
5. **Secretory vesicle → plasma membrane** — vesicle fuses with the membrane; protein is released by exocytosis

### 3. Membrane Transport

**Passive transport (no ATP):**

- Simple diffusion — small nonpolar molecules (O₂, CO₂) move down their concentration gradient
- Facilitated diffusion — polar/charged molecules use channel or carrier proteins (e.g., glucose via GLUT transporter)
- Osmosis — water moves through aquaporins down its concentration gradient

**Active transport (requires ATP):**

- Sodium-potassium pump — moves 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in against gradients
- Endocytosis/exocytosis — bulk transport of large molecules (e.g., phagocytosis of bacteria)
- Cells need both: passive for efficient movement down gradients; active to maintain concentration differences essential for cell function

### 4. Energy Harvest

| Feature | Aerobic Respiration | Fermentation |
|---------|-------------------|--------------|
| Location | Cytoplasm (glycolysis) + mitochondria | Cytoplasm only |
| Oxygen | Required (final electron acceptor) | Not required |
| ATP yield | ~36–38 ATP per glucose | 2 ATP per glucose (net) |
| End products | CO₂ + H₂O | Lactic acid (animals) or ethanol + CO₂ (yeast) |

Fermentation regenerates NAD⁺ so glycolysis can continue without oxygen. It is far less efficient but essential under anaerobic conditions.
