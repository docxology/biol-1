### Module 10: Inheritance

1. When two heterozygous parents (Aa × Aa) are crossed, the expected ratio of phenotypes is:
    - A) 1:1
    - B) 3:1
    - C) 1:2:1
    - D) 4:0

2. If both parents are carriers of a recessive trait (Aa × Aa), what is the chance their child will show the recessive trait?
    - A) 0%
    - B) 25%
    - C) 50%
    - D) 75%

3. In incomplete dominance, the heterozygote shows:
    - A) The dominant trait only
    - B) The recessive trait only
    - C) A blend of both traits
    - D) Neither trait

4. Human blood type is an example of:
    - A) Simple dominance
    - B) Incomplete dominance
    - C) Multiple alleles and codominance
    - D) Polygenic inheritance

5. A "carrier" is a person who:
    - A) Is homozygous dominant
    - B) Is homozygous recessive and shows the trait
    - C) Is heterozygous and does not show the recessive trait
    - D) Has no alleles for the trait

6. In a dihybrid cross (RrYy × RrYy), the expected phenotypic ratio is:
    - A) 3:1
    - B) 1:2:1
    - C) 9:3:3:1
    - D) 1:1:1:1

7. A pedigree shows two unaffected parents with an affected child. This trait is most likely:
    - A) Autosomal dominant
    - B) Autosomal recessive
    - C) X-linked dominant
    - D) Y-linked

8. Males are more often affected by X-linked recessive conditions because:
    - A) Males have two X chromosomes
    - B) Males have one X, so one recessive allele is enough
    - C) Females have a protective Y chromosome
    - D) X-linked traits only come from fathers

9. A carrier mother (X^c X) and a father with normal vision (X Y) have sons. What fraction of their sons will be color blind?
    - A) None
    - B) One-quarter
    - C) One-half
    - D) All of them

10. The Law of Independent Assortment states that:
    - A) Alleles always stay together
    - B) Genes on different chromosomes sort independently during meiosis
    - C) Dominant alleles always mask recessive alleles
    - D) Traits always skip a generation

11. A father with blood type A (genotype AO) and a mother with blood type B (genotype BO) could have a child with blood type:
    - A) A only
    - B) B only
    - C) AB only
    - D) A, B, AB, or O

12. If a trait is autosomal dominant, an affected parent crossed with an unaffected parent will produce:
    - A) All affected children
    - B) About half affected children
    - C) No affected children
    - D) All carrier children

13. Which of the following is true about sex-linked inheritance?
    - A) Fathers pass X-linked traits to their sons
    - B) Mothers can pass X-linked recessive traits to their sons
    - C) X-linked traits affect males and females equally
    - D) Y-linked traits are more common than X-linked traits
