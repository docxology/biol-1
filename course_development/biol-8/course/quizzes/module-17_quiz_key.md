# Module 17 Quiz: Evolution — Answer Key

## Part A: Multiple Choice (7 points)

1. **C) Natural selection**
   *Explanation: Natural selection is the mechanism by which evolutionary change occurs. Lamarck's theory (A and B) was later discredited.*

2. **D) All of the above**
   *Explanation: Natural selection requires variation, the inheritance of that variation, and that some variations provide better reproductive success.*

3. **B) Adaptations**
   *Explanation: Adaptations are traits evolved through natural selection that improve an organism's fitness in its environment.*

4. **B) Homologous structures**
   *Explanation: Homologous structures provide evidence of shared ancestry, even if they now serve different functions.*

5. **C) Analogous structures**
   *Explanation: These are structures that developed independently in different lineages to solve the same functional problem (convergent evolution).*

6. **B) Genetic variation**
   *Explanation: Without genetic variation (from mutation or sexual reproduction), natural selection would have no different traits to select from.*

7. **C) DNA sequence comparisons**
   *Explanation: While other methods provide evidence, DNA sequencing offers the most direct and mathematically precise measurement of common ancestry.*

## Part B: Free Response (3 points)

8. Define the term **Evolution** in a biological context.
   *Answer: Evolution is the change in the inherited characteristics of a population over successive generations.*

9. Provide one example of a **vestigial structure** in humans and explain what it suggests about our ancestry.
   *Answer: Examples include the appendix, tailbone (coccyx), or wisdom teeth. These structures suggest they were once functional in an ancestral species that had different dietary or locomotive needs.*

10. Explain why **Natural Selection** acts on individuals, but only **populations** evolve.
    __________________________________________________________________
    *Answer: Individuals either survive and reproduce or they do not based on their traits. However, an individual cannot change its own genetic makeup; only the frequency of those traits in the entire population changes over time.*
