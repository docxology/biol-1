# Module 10: Tissues

**OpenStax Connection:** Chapter 4 (Anatomy & Physiology) / Chapter 16.3 (Biology 2e)

## Overview

In this module, we move from the level of single cells to **tissues**—groups of similar cells working together to perform a specific function. Tissues are the "fabric" of the human body, building every organ and system we will study for the rest of the course.

We will explore the four primary tissue types that make up the human body:

1. **Epithelial Tissue**: Covers body surfaces, lines cavities, and forms glands. It protects, absorbs, and secretes.
2. **Connective Tissue**: Binds and supports body parts. This diverse group includes bone, cartilage, fat, and even blood.
3. **Muscle Tissue**: Specialized for contraction to produce movement. We will look at skeletal, cardiac, and smooth muscle.
4. **Nervous Tissue**: Specialized for communication via electrical impulses. It includes neurons and support cells (glia).

Understanding tissues is critical because "structure dictates function." The specific arrangement of cells in a tissue determines what that body part can do—and how it fails during disease.

## Learning Goals

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

* Identify the four major tissue types and their general functions.
* Describe the microscopic appearance of common epithelial and connective tissues.
* Explain how the structure of a tissue (e.g., simple squamous vs. stratified squamous) relates to its function (e.g., diffusion vs. protection).
* Compare and contrast the three types of muscle tissue.
* Describe the basic structure and function of nervous tissue.
