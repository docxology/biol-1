*Choose the best answer for each question.*

A) Cuts DNA  
B) Copies DNA  
C) Joins (seals) DNA fragments together  
D) Separates DNA strands

**4.** A plasmid is:

A) A human chromosome  
B) A small circular DNA molecule found in bacteria  
C) A type of RNA  
D) A protein that cuts DNA

**5.** Recombinant DNA is:

A) DNA that has been destroyed  
B) DNA made from two different sources  
C) DNA that has been methylated  
D) Normal bacterial DNA

A) Primers bind to DNA  
B) DNA strands separate  
C) New DNA is synthesized

**7.** In PCR, what happens during the annealing step (~55°C)?

A) DNA strands separate  
B) Primers bind to the target DNA  
C) New DNA strands are built

**8.** Why is Taq polymerase used in PCR instead of normal DNA polymerase?

A) It is faster  
B) It can survive the high heat of the denaturation step  
C) It does not need primers  
D) It works at room temperature

**9.** Starting with 1 DNA molecule, how many copies do you have after 5 PCR cycles?

A) 5  
B) 10  
C) 16  
D) 32

**10.** In gel electrophoresis, DNA migrates toward the:

A) Negative electrode  
B) Positive electrode  
C) Center of the gel  
D) Top of the gel

**11.** In gel electrophoresis, smaller DNA fragments:

A) Stay near the top (wells)  
B) Travel farther from the wells  
C) Don't move at all  
D) Move toward the negative end

**12.** A DNA ladder in gel electrophoresis is used as:

A) A source of restriction enzymes  
B) A size reference (known fragment sizes)  
C) A dye to see the DNA  
D) A primer for PCR

**13.** STRs (Short Tandem Repeats) are used in:

A) PCR  
B) DNA fingerprinting (forensics and paternity tests)  
C) Transcription  
D) Translation

**14.** In a paternity test, which bands in the child must come from the biological father?

A) All bands  
B) The bands that don't match the mother  
C) The largest bands  
D) The smallest bands

**15.** A transgenic organism (GMO) is an organism that:

A) Has had a gene deleted  
B) Contains a gene from another species  
C) Has been exposed to radiation  
D) Is a clone

**16.** To make bacteria produce human insulin, scientists:

A) Feed bacteria insulin  
B) Insert the human insulin gene into a bacterial plasmid  
C) Remove all bacterial genes  
D) Inject insulin into bacteria

**17.** Gene therapy treats disease by:

A) Removing all of a patient's DNA  
B) Introducing a functional copy of a defective gene  
C) Replacing all cells in the body  
D) Changing the patient's diet

**18.** In ex vivo gene therapy:

A) Treatment is delivered directly into the body  
B) Cells are removed, modified in the lab, and returned to the patient  
C) The patient's DNA is entirely replaced

**19.** CRISPR-Cas9 is:

A) A type of restriction enzyme  
B) A precise gene-editing tool that can cut DNA at a specific location  
C) A method of gel electrophoresis

**20.** The difference between somatic gene therapy and germline modification is:

A) Somatic therapy changes gametes; germline changes body cells  
B) Somatic therapy changes body cells only; germline changes are inherited

**21.** Golden Rice is an example of:

A) Natural selection  
B) A transgenic crop designed to provide Vitamin A  
C) A pesticide  
D) Selective breeding

**22.** Why can bacteria read a human gene and make a human protein?

A) Bacteria and humans have the same genes  
B) The genetic code is universal (same codons, same amino acids)

**23.** The Human Genome Project:

A) Created the first transgenic organism

**24.** One ethical concern about germline editing is:

A) It only works in bacteria  
B) Changes are inherited by future generations

D) It is too slow

A) Using chemicals to modify DNA  
B) Using computational tools to analyze biological data
