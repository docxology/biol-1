# Lab 7: Energy, Photosynthesis, & Cellular Respiration

## Overview

Energy is the currency of life. It flows through ecosystems from the sun to producers, then to consumers, and eventually dissipates as heat. In contrast, matter (like carbon, hydrogen, and oxygen) constantly cycles between the environment and living organisms. In this comprehensive lab, you will model the complementary processes of photosynthesis and cellular respiration. Through detailed structural drawing and step-by-step pathway tracking, you will build a robust understanding of how cells capture, transfer, and consume energy.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this lab, you will be able to:

1. Relate the balanced chemical equations for photosynthesis and cellular respiration to the cycling of carbon.
2. Trace the path of energy from sunlight to stored chemical energy (glucose), and finally to usable cellular energy (ATP).
3. Diagram the microscopic structures of the chloroplast and mitochondrion and identify where specific metabolic reactions occur.
4. Model the Electron Transport Chain (ETC) and explain how chemiosmosis generates ATP.
5. Calculate the relative energy yields of aerobic respiration versus anaerobic fermentation under different physiological conditions.
6. Connect the concepts of autotrophy and heterotrophy to global ecological systems.

## Part 4: Fermentation Case Studies

When oxygen is absent, the ETC shuts down, halting the Krebs cycle. Cells must rely on Glycolysis alone, followed by fermentation to regenerate NAD⁺.

### Case Study A: The Sprinter

A track athlete runs a 400-meter dash. Around the 200-meter mark, her muscle cells use up available oxygen faster than her cardiovascular system can deliver it.

* **Pathway:** Her cells switch to Lactic Acid Fermentation.
* **Energy Yield:** Only 2 ATP per glucose molecule.

### Case Study B: The Brewer's Yeast

Yeast cells are sealed in a vat of sugary grape juice with no oxygen.

* **Pathway:** The yeast cells perform Alcoholic Fermentation.
* **Energy Yield:** 2 ATP per glucose, plus Ethanol and CO₂ (bubbles).
