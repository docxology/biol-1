# Module 13: How Populations Evolve

Welcome to Module 13! Now that we understand the basics of natural selection from Module 12, it is time to look at evolution under a microscope—literally. In this module, we will explore **microevolution**: the shift in gene frequencies within a single population over time. You will learn about the five specific forces that drive evolutionary change and how scientists measure it.

## Module Structure

1. **[Keys to Success](keys-to-success.md)**
   - Dive into the mechanisms of evolution (Genetic Drift, Gene Flow, Mutation) and the different ways natural selection can reshape a trait curve over time.
2. **[Practice Questions](questions.md)**
   - Test your ability to identify the various evolutionary forces acting on a biological population.

## Learning Objectives

By completing this module, you should be able to:
- Define microevolution in terms of allele frequencies.
- Describe the five mechanisms of microevolution (Mutation, Gene Flow, Genetic Drift, Non-random Mating, and Natural Selection).
- Contrast the "Bottleneck Effect" and the "Founder Effect."
- Visually identify the three types of natural selection (Directional, Stabilizing, Disruptive).
- Grasp the conceptual role of the Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium as a baseline for non-evolving populations.
