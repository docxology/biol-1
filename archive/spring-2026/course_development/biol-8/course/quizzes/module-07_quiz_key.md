## Part B: Free Response (3 points)

**8. Transcription (1 point)**

- Transcription occurs in the nucleus
- RNA polymerase binds to the promoter region of a gene on DNA
- It reads the template strand 3'→5' and builds a complementary mRNA strand 5'→3'
- Produces a single-stranded mRNA molecule that carries the genetic message to the ribosome
- Uracil (U) replaces thymine (T) in the RNA

**9. Sequence Translation (1 point)**

- DNA template: 3'–TAC TTT GCA ATT–5'
- mRNA sequence: 5'–AUG AAA CGU UAA–3'
- Amino acid sequence: Met – Lys – Arg – Stop
- AUG = Methionine (start), AAA = Lysine, CGU = Arginine, UAA = Stop codon
- Final polypeptide: Met–Lys–Arg (3 amino acids before termination)

**10. Mutation Effects (1 point)**

- A single base substitution changes one codon in the mRNA
- This may code for a different amino acid (missense mutation)
- The altered amino acid can change the protein's shape and function
- Example: In sickle cell anemia, a single base change (GAG → GUG in mRNA) substitutes valine for glutamic acid in hemoglobin
- This causes hemoglobin molecules to aggregate, deforming red blood cells into a sickle shape
- Silent mutations (same amino acid) and nonsense mutations (premature stop) are also possible outcomes
