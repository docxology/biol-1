# Module 16 Quiz: Ecology — Answer Key

## Part A: Multiple Choice (7 points)

1. **B) Community**
   *Explanation: A community consists of all populations of different species in a given area. An ecosystem includes the community plus the abiotic (non-living) environment.*

2. **B) The population size increases at a constant rate per unit of time.**
   *Explanation: Exponential growth is J-shaped; the growth rate is proportional to the population size.*

3. **C) Carrying capacity**
   *Explanation: Carrying capacity (K) is the maximum number of individuals an environment can support sustainably.*

4. **C) Competitive exclusion**
   *Explanation: The principle of competitive exclusion states that two species competing for the same limited resource cannot coexist permanently.*

5. **B) Commensalism**
   *Explanation: Commensalism is a relationship where one organism benefits and the other is unaffected (+/0).*

6. **A) Primary producers**
   *Explanation: Primary producers (plants/algae) sit at the base of the food chain and contain the most energy and total biomass.*

7. **D) All of the above.**
   *Explanation: Most energy is lost as heat, some parts are indigestible, and some organisms survive long enough to avoid being eaten.*

## Part B: Free Response (3 points)

8. Describe one **density-dependent** factor that might limit the growth of a population of deer in a forest.
   *Answer: Common factors include disease, predation, and competition for space or food. As the population density increases, these factors become more restrictive.*

9. Explain the difference between an organism's **habitat** and its **niche**.
   *Answer: A habitat is the physical place where an organism lives (its "address"), while a niche is the functional role it plays in the community (its "profession" or "job").*

10. What is the role of **decomposers** in an ecosystem's nutrient balance?
    __________________________________________________________________
    *Answer: Decomposers break down dead organic matter, returning vital nutrients (such as carbon and nitrogen) to the soil or water so they can be reused by primary producers. Without them, nutrients would remain locked in organic waste.*
